Which ETFs Hold Globalstar ($GSAT) Stock? The Full List for 2026

Three space funds hold more than 5% Globalstar, two telecom funds and $UFO hold about 3%, and the biggest space ETF doesn't hold it at all. Weights, fees, and what Amazon's pending $90 a share deal means for every fund on this list.

From Our Network
Quick answer: The ETFs with the most Globalstar ($GSAT) are $MARS (Roundhill Space & Technology ETF) at about 5.8%, $ORBX (Global X Space Tech ETF) at about 5.3% and $WARP (VanEck Space ETF) at about 5.1%. The telecom fund $XTL holds about 3.5%, $UFO about 3.3% and $IYZ about 3.0%. $NASA, $ARKX and $ROKT don't show it in their top 25. Weights are as of November 10 to 13, 2026 and change daily.

My lists of ETFs that hold Iridium, EchoStar and AST SpaceMobile cover the satellite names people search for most. Globalstar is the one I kept skipping, and it shouldn't be. It's a top 10 holding in four of the space funds I track, and in $MARS it's number 3, right behind SpaceX and Rocket Lab.

Like Iridium, Globalstar is a deal stock now. Amazon agreed to buy Globalstar on April 13, 2026 (announced April 14) for $90.00 per share in cash or 0.3210 Amazon shares, with the stock option capped at $90.00 in value. Thermo-affiliated holders with about 58% of the vote already signed off, so there's no shareholder vote. The deal is expected to close in 2027, once regulators sign off and Globalstar hits certain satellite milestones.

$GSAT closed at $86.24 on November 12, 2026, a market value of about $11.2 billion. Here's every way to own it in a fund, and what happens to those funds when Amazon closes the deal.

$ORBX Global X Space Tech ETF

$UFO Procure Space ETF

The oldest pure space fund, launched April 11, 2019, and still one of the biggest at about $451 million. Globalstar is 3.32%, its 9th largest. What You're Actually Buying With Globalstar

Before I buy a fund for one name, I want to know what that name actually is. Quick version:

  • The business: Globalstar runs a low Earth orbit satellite network for mobile satellite services: voice and data, SPOT personal trackers, and a fast-growing Commercial IoT business. It also owns valuable MSS spectrum licenses, including Band 53/n53.
  • The Apple connection: Globalstar's network powers satellite features on iPhone 14 and later and Apple Watch Ultra 3. Apple agreed in 2024 to take a 20% stake in the company. In the Amazon deal, Amazon says it will keep supporting those Apple devices.
  • The numbers: Q3 2026 revenue was $70.9 million, including $65.4 million of service revenue and $5.5 million of equipment sales. Service revenue fell $4.2 million from a year earlier, mostly from timing on wholesale capacity fees, while Commercial IoT kept growing and equipment sales rose 31%.
  • The satellites: Eight replacement satellites built by MDA Space launched on a SpaceX Falcon 9 in August 2026. On November 10, MDA said all eight had finished initial in-orbit tests and started raising their orbits. They're part of a 17-satellite order.
  • The next network: Globalstar is building a third generation constellation called C-3 and got a key FCC authorization for it in the third quarter.

This is the part most people miss about Globalstar. On revenue alone, roughly $280 million a year, an $11 billion price tag looks crazy. What Amazon is paying for is the spectrum, the licenses and the Apple relationship, not this year's sales.

The Amazon Deal and What It Means for These ETFs

Here's how the deal works, straight from Amazon's announcement and the merger filings:

  • The price: Each $GSAT share can elect $90.00 in cash or 0.3210 Amazon shares, with the stock value capped at $90.00. If Amazon trades below about $280.38, the stock option is worth less than $90.
  • The catch on cash: Cash elections are capped at 40% of the shares. If more people want cash, it gets prorated and the rest is paid in Amazon stock. Anyone who doesn't elect gets stock.
  • A small haircut: The payout is reduced by a per-share adjustment tied to Globalstar's agreement with Apple on C-3 milestones. It was capped at $110 million total and the August filing estimated about $97 million, roughly $0.75 a share.
  • Timing: Expected to close in 2027. The FCC Space Bureau approved the license transfer in October 2026, but other regulatory approvals and satellite milestones are still pending.
Why $GSAT trades below $90: Amazon closed at $267.44 on November 12, 2026. At that price, 0.3210 Amazon shares are worth about $85.85, and most of the deal will be paid in stock because cash is capped at 40%. That's why $GSAT sits around $86. The stock now moves mostly with Amazon, not with anything Globalstar does on its own. And if the deal falls apart, there's no guarantee the price holds.

For the ETFs, this means a few things. When the deal closes, Globalstar stops trading, and each fund gets cash and Amazon shares for its $GSAT. Space funds generally aren't built to hold Amazon, so I'd expect most to sell or rebalance those shares, but how each one handles it is up to its index or manager.

Until then, the Globalstar slice of these funds behaves like a capped deal position. It can't run much past $90, so for upside in the space funds you're relying on SpaceX, Rocket Lab and AST SpaceMobile, not Globalstar.

Does Globalstar Pay a Dividend?

No. StockAnalysis.com lists no common dividend for $GSAT, and the company has been reinvesting in satellites and ground stations, not paying shareholders. Space ETFs aren't income funds either. Most of them pay little or nothing.

That's fine for the growth side of a portfolio. Just don't confuse it with income. If you want companies that actually pay you, that's what TopDividendETFs.com is for.

Which Approach Fits You

  • You want the phone-to-satellite theme in one fund: $ORBX, with Globalstar, AST SpaceMobile and Iridium all in its top 10, at 0.50%.
  • You want the most Globalstar: $MARS at 5.80%. Just know you're mostly buying SpaceX and Rocket Lab.
  • You want satellite names inside a cheaper, broad telecom fund: $XTL at 0.35%.
  • You want an established space fund with a long track record: $UFO, launched in 2019, about $451 million in assets.
  • You want Globalstar exposure on purpose: no ETF gives you much. And with the deal pending, owning $GSAT here is mostly a bet on Amazon stock and the deal closing.

My Take

I'm a dividend investor first. I reached financial freedom by building income, not by betting on satellite deals. But Globalstar is a good example of why I always look under the hood of a fund before I buy it.

If you bought a space ETF this year hoping Globalstar would be a big winner, that story mostly played out when Amazon showed up. From here the position is close to capped. The real swing factors in $MARS, $ORBX and $WARP are SpaceX and Rocket Lab. If that's what you want, fine, just own it on purpose.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which ETF has the most Globalstar stock?

The Roundhill Space & Technology ETF ($MARS) had the biggest Globalstar weight at about 5.80% as of November 13, 2026, followed by the Global X Space Tech ETF ($ORBX) at about 5.31% and the VanEck Space ETF ($WARP) at about 5.11%. $XTL held about 3.53%, $UFO about 3.32% and $IYZ about 3.04%.

Is Amazon buying Globalstar?

Yes. Amazon agreed on April 13, 2026 to acquire Globalstar for $90.00 per share in cash or 0.3210 Amazon shares, with the stock value capped at $90.00 and cash elections capped at 40% of shares. Holders with about 58% of the vote approved it by written consent. The deal is expected to close in 2027, subject to regulatory approvals and satellite milestones.

What happens to ETFs that hold Globalstar when the deal closes?

Globalstar would stop trading as a separate stock, and funds holding $GSAT would receive cash and Amazon shares under the merger terms. How each fund then handles the Amazon shares depends on its index or manager.

Does Globalstar pay a dividend?

No. Globalstar does not pay a dividend on its common stock as of November 2026.

Does the ARK space ETF hold Globalstar?

The ARK Space and Defense Innovation ETF ($ARKX) did not show Globalstar among its top 25 holdings as of November 12, 2026, though it held about 4.98% Amazon. The Tema Space Innovators ETF ($NASA) and $ROKT also did not show it in their top 25.
