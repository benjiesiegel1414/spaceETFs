Which ETFs Hold SpaceX (SPCX) Stock? The Full List for 2026

July 18, 2026·10 min read·By the TopSpaceETFs Team

Quick answer: As of July 2026, roughly 179 US ETFs hold SpaceX (NASDAQ: SPCX) stock, collectively owning around $16 billion worth of shares. The biggest holders by dollar value are the Nasdaq-100 trackers QQQ and QQQM, which account for nearly half of all ETF-held SpaceX stock. Below, we break down every category of SpaceX ETF — from broad index funds you may already own, to concentrated crossover funds, to 2x leveraged single-stock ETFs.

Key Takeaway

You don't need to buy SPCX directly to own SpaceX. If you hold QQQ, QQQM, VTI, or a Russell 1000 index fund, you already own a slice — and if you want more exposure than the index gives you, there are space ETFs, crossover funds, and leveraged single-stock ETFs built for exactly that.

First, a Quick Recap: The SPCX IPO

SpaceX completed the largest IPO in history on June 12, 2026, pricing at $135 per share, opening its first trade at $150, and closing day one at $160.95 — a roughly 19% first-day pop that briefly valued the company around $2.1 trillion.

Since then, gravity has kicked in. The stock has been volatile through its first month of trading and slipped below its $135 IPO price in mid-July 2026, even after being added to the Nasdaq-100. That volatility is precisely why so many investors are searching for ETF exposure instead of holding the single stock: an ETF wrapper dilutes the day-to-day whiplash of one of the most hyped debuts ever.

For the full IPO story, see our breakdown: SpaceX Is Now Public (SPCX): What It Means for Space ETF Investors.

Category 1: Broad Index ETFs That Now Hold SpaceX

This is the category most people don't realize they're in. When SpaceX went public, index providers had a decision to make — and they didn't all make the same one.

Nasdaq and FTSE Russell fast-tracked SpaceX into their indexes. S&P Dow Jones Indices did not change its methodology, which means SpaceX is NOT in the S&P 500 as of this writing. If you own SPY or VOO, you don't own SpaceX yet.

Here's who does hold it:

Invesco Nasdaq 100 ETF QQQM Nasdaq-100 Same index, lower fee

Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF VTI CRSP US Total Market Small weight, added June 18, 2026

One important nuance: only a small percentage of SpaceX's total shares actually trade publicly (estimates put the free float in the low single digits). Index rules cap SPCX's weight based on that limited float, which is why its position in broad funds is smaller than its headline market cap would suggest. Translation: owning VTI gives you SpaceX exposure, but not much of it.

Category 2: Dedicated Space ETFs

If you want SpaceX inside a basket of launch providers, satellite operators, and space infrastructure companies — the whole space economy, not just one rocket company — this is the category built for it. These are the funds we track daily on our Top Space ETFs rankings:

  • Procure Space ETF (UFO) — the original space-focused ETF, covering satellite communications, launch services, and space infrastructure across the sector.
  • Themed space funds like NASA and ARKX — actively watched space-economy funds that give diversified exposure to the theme alongside SpaceX-adjacent names.

The trade-off: dedicated space ETFs typically carry higher expense ratios than broad index funds, and their SpaceX weighting varies fund to fund. Check each fund's current holdings before assuming how much SPCX you're actually getting — weightings in this category are moving frequently while the stock finds its footing.

Category 3: Crossover Funds With Big SpaceX Positions

These funds are where the concentrated (but still diversified) SpaceX exposure lives. Several got in before the IPO through rules allowing registered funds to hold limited private positions — so they were already sitting on SpaceX when it listed.

ETF Ticker SpaceX Weight Notes
Baron First Principles ETF RONB ~30% ~$240M AUM, 26 holdings, SpaceX is top position
ERShares Private-Public Crossover ETF XOVR ~17% ~$460M AUM, 33 holdings, 0.75% expense ratio

These weightings are dramatically higher than anything in the index or space-ETF categories. That cuts both ways: more upside if SpaceX runs, and more pain on days like the ones we've seen this month. Expense ratios in this category also run high — often 0.75% to 1.00% versus 0.03% for a broad-market fund.

Category 4: Leveraged Single-Stock SpaceX ETFs

Within days of the IPO, issuers launched single-stock ETFs targeting 2x SpaceX's daily move:

  • ProShares Ultra SpaceX (SPCF) — seeks 2x the daily performance of SPCX.
  • Defiance Daily Target 2X Long SPCX ETF (SPCU) — same 2x daily objective from Defiance.
  • GraniteShares SpaceX leveraged ETFs — leveraged SPCX exposure from GraniteShares' single-stock lineup.

A blunt warning that applies to this entire category: these are daily-reset trading products, not buy-and-hold investments. Because leverage resets each day, returns over any period longer than one day can differ substantially from 2x the stock's move — and volatility (which SPCX has in abundance right now) actively erodes returns over time. These exist for short-term traders who understand the mechanics. If that sentence doesn't describe you, this category probably isn't for you.

So Which Approach Makes Sense?

It depends entirely on how much SpaceX you actually want — and every option carries real risk:

  • "A little, passively": You may already have it. QQQ, QQQM, VTI, IWB, and VONE holders own SpaceX automatically at a small index weight.
  • "SpaceX plus the whole space economy": Dedicated space ETFs like UFO spread the bet across the sector. Compare them side-by-side on our rankings table.
  • "A lot, but still in a fund": Crossover funds like XOVR and RONB carry SpaceX at double-digit weightings — with fees and volatility to match.
  • "Maximum exposure, short-term": Leveraged single-stock ETFs — for experienced traders only.
Worth Remembering

SPCX fell below its $135 IPO price barely a month after the biggest debut in market history. Hype and volatility travel together. Whatever exposure route you're considering, size it like the highly speculative position it is — and talk to a licensed financial advisor first.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does QQQ hold SpaceX stock?

Yes. SpaceX was fast-tracked into the Nasdaq-100 after its June 2026 IPO, so QQQ and QQQM both hold SPCX — roughly a 1.2% combined stake worth over $7 billion as of mid-July 2026.

Is SpaceX in the S&P 500?

Not yet. S&P Dow Jones Indices declined to change its methodology to fast-track SpaceX, so S&P 500 funds like SPY and VOO don't hold SPCX as of this writing. Nasdaq and FTSE Russell indexes did fast-track it.

Which ETF has the most SpaceX exposure?

Among diversified funds, Baron's RONB held SpaceX at roughly a 30% weighting in mid-2026 and ERShares' XOVR at about 17%. For concentrated exposure, 2x leveraged single-stock ETFs (SPCF, SPCU) exist — but those are daily trading products with significant risk.

Do I already own SpaceX through my index funds?

If you own Nasdaq-100, Russell 1000, or total-market index funds like VTI — yes, a small slice. Because SpaceX's public float is tiny, its weight in broad funds is modest.

How many ETFs hold SpaceX?

As of July 2026, roughly 179 US ETFs held SPCX, worth about $16 billion combined. Nearly half of that sits in QQQ and QQQM alone.
