Which ETFs Hold MDA Space ($MDA) Stock? The Full List for 2026

Five space ETFs own MDA Space, and unlike most of the small names I cover, it's a real top 10 holding in all of them. Weights, fees, what the Canadian satellite and Canadarm maker actually does, its latest numbers and the risks.

From Our Network
Quick answer: The ETFs that hold the most MDA Space ($MDA) are $ORBX (Global X Space Tech ETF) at about 5.2%, $WARP (VanEck Space ETF) at about 5.1%, $NASA (Tema Space Innovators ETF) at about 4.5%, $MARS (Roundhill Space & Technology ETF) at about 3.6% and $UFO (Procure Space ETF) at about 3.2%. $ARKX, $ROKT and the big defense funds don't show it in their top 25. Weights are as of November 17 to 19, 2026 and change daily.

My lists of ETFs that hold SpaceX, Rocket Lab and Globalstar keep pulling in readers, and one name kept showing up in the top 10 of almost every space fund I looked at: MDA Space. Most people in the US have never heard of it. They've probably seen its work, though. MDA's robotics team is behind Canadarm2 on the International Space Station, and today it's working on Canadarm3, the next generation of that arm.

Today MDA is mostly a satellite builder. It's the prime contractor on Globalstar's replacement satellites, its biggest growth driver is Telesat's Lightspeed constellation, and it just got picked for parts of Europe's IRIS² network. It's based in Canada, trades on the Toronto Stock Exchange, and since March 2026 it also trades on the New York Stock Exchange under the same ticker, $MDA.

$MDA closed at $26.76 on the NYSE on November 19, 2026 (C$38.44 in Toronto), a market value of about C$6.2 billion, roughly US$4.4 billion. Its US 52-week range is $15.56 to $49.37. Here's every ETF that owns it, and how much you actually get.

What You're Actually Buying With MDA Space

MDA Space was founded in 1969 and has about 4,000 employees. It reports in Canadian dollars and splits its business into three parts. Here's what each one brought in during the third quarter of 2026:

  • Satellite Systems, C$358.3 million (up 26.4%): building whole satellites and the electronics inside them. The growth came mainly from Telesat's Lightspeed constellation. Its AURORA product line is a software-defined digital satellite, and in 2026 it opened a new high-volume satellite factory and started building Globalstar's replacement satellites (I covered that in my Globalstar ETF list).
  • Robotics and Space Operations, C$79.9 million (up 2.0%): the Canadarm business. The steady work right now is the Canadarm3 program, which MDA says is running at a steady volume.
  • Geointelligence, C$59.9 million (up 24.8%): Earth observation data and analytics. MDA says it's on schedule to launch its own next-generation imaging constellation, MDA CHORUS, by the end of 2026.

Here's how I think about it. Most "space stocks" are one bet: a rocket, a constellation, a lunar lander. MDA is more like a contractor. It gets paid to build other people's constellations, which means more predictable revenue and a big backlog, but it also lives and dies by a handful of very large contracts.

This is the part most people miss. The quarter looked great: revenue up 21.5%, guidance raised to C$1.90 to C$1.95 billion, and about C$1.33 billion of net cash after its NYSE listing, a July stock offering and an August bond deal. But backlog is down, from C$4.4 billion a year ago to C$3.9 billion. MDA is working through its big contracts faster than it's replacing them. That's why the next few contract wins, like IRIS², matter so much. And the cash pile is already spoken for: MDA says it plans to use it to buy Blue Canyon Technologies and CLS (Collecte Localisation Satellites), two deals that haven't closed yet.

The Risks I'd Think About

  • Volatility: a US 52-week range of $15.56 to $49.37. In Toronto the stock hit C$67.90 at its high and closed at C$38.44 on November 19, 2026, about 43% below that peak.
  • Customer concentration: a lot of today's revenue comes from a few huge programs like Telesat Lightspeed and Globalstar. A delay or cancellation would hurt.
  • Shrinking backlog: C$3.9 billion now versus C$4.4 billion a year ago. Growth depends on new wins.
  • Dilution and deals: shares outstanding rose about 9.9% over the past year, and two pending acquisitions bring integration risk.
  • Currency: MDA reports in Canadian dollars, so US investors also carry CAD exchange rate risk.
  • Fund concentration: every ETF that holds MDA is still mostly a SpaceX and Rocket Lab fund. If those two fall, a 5% MDA slice won't save you.

Does MDA Space Pay a Dividend?

No. MDA Space doesn't pay a dividend as of November 2026, and none of the five ETFs above is built for income. Which Approach Fits You

  • You want the most MDA in one fund: $ORBX at 5.21%, with a 0.50% fee.
  • You want a low fee with almost the same MDA weight: $WARP at 5.06%, also 0.50%.
  • You want the biggest, most liquid new space fund: $NASA at 4.46%, with about $873 million in assets.
  • You want MDA without betting everything on SpaceX: $UFO, where SpaceX is about 15.5% and the rest is spread across satellite services and aerospace.
  • You want real MDA exposure on purpose: since its March 2026 NYSE listing, US investors can buy $MDA directly. Size it like a single stock, not a fund.

My Take

I'm a dividend investor first. I reached financial freedom by building income, not by guessing which satellite maker lands the next constellation. But MDA is one of the more interesting names I've looked at in this series, because it's a business with real revenue, positive free cash flow last quarter and a pile of net cash, not just a story.

It's also a good reminder to look inside a fund before you buy it. If you own $ORBX or $WARP, you already own about 5% MDA, whether you knew it or not. Just know that the backlog trend and those two pending acquisitions are what I'd be watching, and keep any single space stock to a size you can sit through a 40% drop with, because this one already did that in the past year.

Which ETF has the most MDA Space stock?
