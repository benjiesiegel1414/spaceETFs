Which ETFs Hold Viasat ($VSAT) Stock? The Full List for 2026

Six ETFs hold real Viasat positions, and five of them land in the same tight 4.4% to 5.2% range. That changes how you pick one. Weights, fees, the SpaceX catch, and the one space fund that skips SpaceX entirely.

Quick answer: The ETF with the biggest Viasat ($VSAT) position is $WARP (VanEck Space ETF) at about 5.2%. Right behind it are $NASA (about 5.1%), $MARS (about 5.0%), $ORBX (about 4.9%) and $UFO (about 4.4%). $ROKT holds about 2.7%. The ARK funds and the big defense ETFs don't show it in their top 25. I couldn't find a single-stock or leveraged Viasat ETF. Weights are as of November 5 and 6, 2026 and change daily.

My lists of ETFs that hold SpaceX, AST SpaceMobile, Rocket Lab and Redwire keep getting found on Google, so here's the next one: Viasat.

Viasat is the old pro of this group. It's not a 2021 SPAC. It went public in December 1996, has about 7,000 employees, and pulls in about $4.66 billion a year in revenue. It runs satellite broadband, in-flight Wi-Fi for airlines, and a big government and defense communications business.

The stock still moves like a space stock, though. $VSAT closed at $63.95 on November 6, 2026, up 7.70% that day. It's up about 69% over the last 52 weeks, but the range was $29.13 to $93.03, and it reported second quarter results on November 5. Market cap is about $8.9 billion. When a stock can swing 3x inside a year, owning it inside a basket starts to make a lot of sense. Here's every way to do it.

Five Funds, Almost the Same Weight: How I'd Break the Tie

When five funds hold roughly 5% Viasat, here's what I actually look at instead:

  • The fee. $WARP and $ORBX charge 0.50%. $NASA, $MARS and $UFO charge 0.75%. On $10,000 that's $50 a year versus $75 a year. Small, but it shows up every single year, and on a theme that can be flat for long stretches, fees eat a bigger share of your return.
  • The SpaceX weight. This is the big one. It ranges from about 14% in $UFO to about 25% in $MARS and $NASA. Your returns in these funds will track SpaceX far more than Viasat.
  • Size and track record. $UFO (2019) and $ROKT (2018) have years of history. $WARP, $ORBX and $MARS are 2026 launches with under $40 million each. Small funds can trade with wider spreads and sometimes close. $NASA is new but already big.
  • The neighbors. $WARP pairs Viasat with EchoStar, Globalstar and Iridium. $UFO pairs it with Sirius XM and Garmin. Same Viasat weight, very different fund.

What You're Actually Buying With Viasat

Before I buy a fund for one name, I want to know what that name actually is. Quick version:

  • The business: two segments. Communication Services covers satellite broadband, in-flight connectivity for airlines, maritime and enterprise connectivity. Defense and Advanced Technologies covers tactical communications for the military, encryption, cybersecurity and space systems. It designs and builds its own GEO, MEO and LEO satellites and payloads, including the ViaSat-3 fleet.
  • The latest quarter: on November 5, 2026, Viasat reported second quarter fiscal 2027 revenue of about $1.18 billion, up about 3%, and a loss of $0.32 per share. Defense and Advanced Technologies grew about 20%, while Communication Services stayed under pressure.
  • The outlook: management still expects mid-single-digit revenue growth for fiscal 2027, roughly flat adjusted EBITDA and free cash flow above $225 million, as ViaSat-3 shifts toward making money.
  • The balance sheet: a market cap of about $8.9 billion but an enterprise value of about $14.0 billion, which tells you there's a lot of debt in the picture. Short interest was about 8.6% of shares.
  • The basics: headquartered in Carlsbad, California, co-founder Mark Dankberg is Chairman and CEO, listed on Nasdaq under $VSAT. No dividend.

So it's a real, large business with a growing defense side, but slow overall growth, heavy debt and a lot of competition in satellite broadband. That mix is exactly why I'd rather spread the risk than bet on it alone.

Which Approach Fits You

  • "I want the most Viasat for the lowest fee." $WARP, 5.20% at 0.50%. $ORBX is close at 4.87% for the same fee.
  • "I want a big, liquid fund." $NASA at about $877 million or $UFO at about $443 million. Both charge 0.75%.
  • "I want a long track record." $UFO (2019) or $ROKT (2018).
  • "I want Viasat but not a giant SpaceX bet." $ROKT, the only fund here without SpaceX in its top 25, and the cheapest at 0.45%. You get about half the Viasat weight.
  • "I own $ARKX, $ARKQ, $ITA or $XAR." You don't have meaningful Viasat exposure. Just know it.

My Take

I'm a dividend investor first. I reached financial freedom by building income, not by betting on space stocks. Viasat doesn't pay a dividend, and with this much debt and a big satellite fleet to pay for, I wouldn't expect one soon.

So if I wanted exposure, I'd keep it small and get it through a basket. Since the Viasat weights are nearly tied, I'd pick based on fee and on how much SpaceX I actually want. For the most Viasat per dollar of fees, $WARP. For a space fund that isn't secretly a SpaceX fund, $ROKT.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which ETF has the most Viasat stock?

The VanEck Space ETF ($WARP) had the biggest Viasat weight at about 5.20% as of November 6, 2026. The Tema Space Innovators ETF ($NASA, about 5.06%), Roundhill's $MARS (about 4.99%) and Global X's $ORBX (about 4.87%) were close behind, with Procure's $UFO at about 4.39%.

Is there a Viasat ETF?

There is no ETF that holds only Viasat, and no single-stock or leveraged Viasat ETF appeared in StockAnalysis.com's database as of November 6, 2026. The easiest way to own it in a fund is a space ETF like $WARP, $NASA, $MARS or $ORBX, which each hold roughly 5% Viasat.

Does $UFO hold Viasat?

Yes. Viasat was about 4.39% of the Procure Space ETF ($UFO) as of November 5, 2026, its 6th largest holding. SpaceX was its largest holding at about 14.29%.

Does $ARKX own Viasat?

Viasat did not appear among the top 25 holdings of the ARK Space & Defense Innovation ETF ($ARKX) in StockAnalysis.com data from early November 2026. Its largest holding was SpaceX at about 10.44%.

Does Viasat stock pay a dividend?

No. Viasat does not pay a dividend. It closed at $63.95 on November 6, 2026, with a market cap of about $8.9 billion and a 52-week range of $29.13 to $93.03.
