Which ETFs Hold Iridium ($IRDM) Stock? The Full List for 2026

Two space funds hold close to 5% Iridium, three more hold 3% to 4%, and a few small cap and telecom funds hold it too. Weights, fees, the dividend, and what Rocket Lab's pending $54 deal means for every fund on this list.

From Our Network
Quick answer: The space ETFs with the most Iridium ($IRDM) are $ORBX (Global X Space Tech ETF) at about 4.8% and $WARP (VanEck Space ETF) at about 4.7%. $MARS and $ROKT hold about 3.6% each and $UFO about 3.1%. Outside of space, the small cap fund $PSCU holds about 4.8% and the telecom funds $XTL and $IYZ hold about 3.3% and 2.8%. $NASA and $ARKX don't show it in their top 25. Weights are as of November 6 to 11, 2026 and change daily.

My lists of ETFs that hold SpaceX, Rocket Lab and EchoStar keep getting found on Google. Iridium shows up inside almost every space fund I pull holdings for, usually sitting somewhere between number 5 and number 10.

And Iridium is a special case right now. Rocket Lab agreed to buy Iridium on June 28, 2026, for $27.00 in cash plus Rocket Lab stock per share, a deal with a headline value of $54.00 per share. Iridium shareholders approved it on September 24 with 99.6% of the votes cast. It's expected to close by mid-2027, once the remaining regulatory approvals come through.

That changes what owning Iridium through a fund actually means, and most people searching "IRDM ETF" don't know it yet. $IRDM was trading around $47 on November 11, 2026, a market value of about $5 billion. Here's every way to own it in a fund, and what happens to it when the deal closes.

What You're Actually Buying With Iridium

Before I buy a fund for one name, I want to know what that name actually is. Quick version:

  • The business: Iridium runs a low Earth orbit satellite network that covers the entire planet, including the oceans and the poles. It sells satellite voice and data, IoT connectivity, and positioning, navigation and timing (PNT) services.
  • The numbers: In Q3 2026, revenue was $245.7 million, up 8% from a year earlier. Service revenue was $187.5 million, up 13%.
  • The government piece: The U.S. government is Iridium's largest single customer at about 15% of service revenue. On September 11, 2026, the U.S. Space Force extended its main contract with Iridium through March 2027.
  • Recent moves: Iridium completed its acquisition of Aireon, the space-based aircraft tracking company, in the third quarter.
  • The cash flow: In 2025, Iridium generated $871.7 million in revenue and $495 million of OEBITDA, a 57% margin, according to the Rocket Lab deal announcement. That's why a "cash cows" fund holds it.

This is the part most people miss about Iridium. It isn't a story stock waiting on a launch. It's a mature, profitable satellite business with a network already in orbit. That's exactly what Rocket Lab said it wanted.

The Rocket Lab Deal and What It Means for These ETFs

Here are the terms, straight from the June 29, 2026 announcement:

  • Iridium shareholders get $27.00 in cash plus Rocket Lab shares for each Iridium share.
  • The stock part uses an exchange ratio with a collar from $67.50 to $112.50 on Rocket Lab's price.
  • The notional value is $54.00 per share, an enterprise value of about $8.0 billion for Iridium.
  • Shareholders approved it on September 24, 2026. Closing is expected by mid-2027, subject to regulatory approvals.

So why is $IRDM around $47 and not $54? The market is pricing in the months until closing, the chance regulators slow it down, and where Rocket Lab's stock ends up. Rocket Lab was around $66 on November 7, near the bottom of that collar band. That gap is what merger arbitrage traders try to capture, and it's not something I'd try to play inside a fund.

Here's what this means for your ETF: once the deal closes, Iridium stops existing as a separate stock. Funds that hold it will get cash plus Rocket Lab shares. If you own $WARP, $ORBX or $MARS, you already hold 9.7% to 13.3% Rocket Lab, so after closing your fund ends up with even more of one company. If you buy a fund today specifically for Iridium, you're really buying a future slice of Rocket Lab.

Does Iridium Pay a Dividend?

Yes, and that's rare in the space world. Iridium paid $0.15 per share for the third quarter of 2026, and the board plans to raise it to $0.16 per share starting with the fourth quarter dividend. That's about a 1.3% yield at today's price. Not an income stock by my standards, but a real, growing payout.

One thing to keep in mind: Rocket Lab doesn't pay a dividend. Once the deal closes, that Iridium dividend goes away, and the funds holding it lose that small trickle of income.

Which Approach Fits You

  • "I want the most Iridium in a space fund." $ORBX at 4.83% or $WARP at 4.67%, both at 0.50%. Both are small funds launched in 2026.
  • "I want Iridium without stacking more Rocket Lab and SpaceX." $ROKT, 3.59% at 0.45%, with neither in its top 25.
  • "I want the cheapest Iridium exposure." $PSCU at 0.29%, but it's a $17 million small cap utilities fund, so know what else you're buying.
  • "I want a space fund with a track record." $UFO, 3.10%, launched in 2019.
  • "I'm actually betting on the combined Rocket Lab and Iridium." $WARP has the most of both, almost 18% together.

My Take

I'm a dividend investor first. I reached financial freedom by building income, not by betting on space deals. But Iridium is one of the few space companies I'd call a real business: global network in orbit, high margins, government contracts and a growing dividend.

The problem is timing. With the Rocket Lab deal approved and expected to close by mid-2027, buying a fund for Iridium today is really a bet on Rocket Lab. If that's what you want, I'd just own it on purpose through $WARP or $ORBX. If you want the steady satellite business without more Rocket Lab on top, $ROKT is the cleaner pick.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which ETF has the most Iridium stock?

Among space funds, the Global X Space Tech ETF ($ORBX) had the biggest Iridium weight at about 4.83% as of November 9, 2026, followed by the VanEck Space ETF ($WARP) at about 4.67%. The small cap fund $PSCU held about 4.80%. $MARS and $ROKT held about 3.6% each and $UFO about 3.1%.

Is Rocket Lab buying Iridium?

Yes. Rocket Lab ($RKLB) agreed on June 28, 2026 to acquire Iridium for $27.00 in cash plus Rocket Lab stock per share, a notional value of $54.00 per share and an enterprise value of about $8.0 billion. Iridium shareholders approved the deal on September 24, 2026, and it is expected to close by mid-2027, subject to regulatory approvals.

What happens to ETFs that hold Iridium when the deal closes?

Iridium would stop trading as a separate stock. Funds holding $IRDM would receive the cash and Rocket Lab shares from the deal. Funds that already own Rocket Lab, like $WARP, $ORBX and $MARS, would end up with a larger Rocket Lab position.

Does Iridium pay a dividend?

Yes. Iridium paid $0.15 per share for the third quarter of 2026, and its board plans to raise the quarterly dividend to $0.16 per share starting with the fourth quarter 2026 dividend. That is a yield of roughly 1.3% at November 2026 prices.

Does the ARK space ETF hold Iridium?

The ARK Space and Defense Innovation ETF ($ARKX) did not show Iridium among its top 25 holdings as of November 10, 2026. The Tema Space Innovators ETF ($NASA) also did not show it in its top 25, though $NASA holds about 10.7% Rocket Lab.
