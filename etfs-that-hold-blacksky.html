Which ETFs Hold BlackSky ($BKSY) Stock? The Full List for 2026

Three space funds hold BlackSky, the biggest position is under 3%, and several popular space and defense ETFs skip it. Weights, fees, what the satellite intelligence company actually sells, and why its record quarter came with a big caveat.

Weights are as of November 13 to 18, 2026 and change daily.

My lists of ETFs that hold Rocket Lab, Intuitive Machines and Planet Labs keep pulling in readers, so here's the next one people ask about. BlackSky is the other big US satellite imaging name, and it just had the best quarter in its history.

BlackSky doesn't launch rockets or sell phone service from space. It runs a constellation of small imaging satellites and sells what they see, mostly to defense and intelligence customers, with AI software that flags what changed at a port, an airfield or a factory. That makes it a "space data" stock, which is why it shows up in the newer space funds but not in the classic defense funds.

$BKSY closed at $21.90 on November 17, 2026, a market value of about $907 million. Its 52-week range is $12.62 to $52.88, so this one moves. Here's every way to own it in a fund, and how little of it you actually get.

What You're Actually Buying With BlackSky

BlackSky Technology is based in Herndon, Virginia. It went public in September 2021 by merging with Osprey Technology Acquisition Corp., a SPAC, and it did a 1-for-8 reverse split in September 2024. The business comes down to three things:

  • Space-based intelligence and AI services: subscriptions to imagery from its own satellites, run through its Spectra AI software to spot changes at ports, airports, construction sites and other key locations. This is the part that's growing fastest.
  • Gen-3 satellites: its newest, sharper satellites. BlackSky says it deployed its fifth Gen-3 satellite in the third quarter, and most of its recent contract wins are "Gen-3 Assured" subscriptions for defense customers.
  • Mission solutions: building satellites and systems for other governments. BlackSky says it was named exclusive provider of very-high resolution optical satellites under a $1 billion program through a consortium that includes France and the UAE, for an initial constellation of 50 satellites.

Here's how I think about it. BlackSky is a picture-and-analytics subscription business wrapped around a fleet of satellites. When demand shows up, margins can be great because the satellites are already in orbit. When it doesn't, you're stuck paying for the fleet.

This is the part most people miss. The headline said "net income of $19 million," but BlackSky's own release says that swing was driven partly by gains on its warrants and other equity instruments that are marked to market. The cleaner signal is revenue up 118% and record adjusted EBITDA. Over the last 12 months the company still burned about $71 million in free cash flow, mostly on satellites. Real progress, not a finished story.

The Risks I'd Think About

  • Volatility: a 52-week range of $12.62 to $52.88. The stock closed as high as $51.63 in late May 2026 and as low as $16.64 on November 2, 2026, three days before that record quarter. That's a 68% drop from the high, inside one year.
  • Dilution: shares outstanding rose about 17% over the past year. Satellites are expensive, and growing space companies often pay for them by issuing stock.
  • Government customers: most of the demand comes from defense and intelligence agencies at home and abroad. Budgets and politics can shift fast.
  • Competition: imaging is a crowded field. Planet Labs, Maxar and a long list of newer constellations all chase the same customers.
  • Short interest: about 16% of shares were sold short in the latest data. That can add fuel in both directions.
  • Fund concentration: the ETFs that hold BlackSky are mostly bets on SpaceX and Rocket Lab. If those two fall, a 2% BlackSky slice won't save you.

Does BlackSky Pay a Dividend?

No. BlackSky doesn't pay a dividend as of November 2026, and none of the three ETFs above is built for income. That's the honest truth about the whole space theme: it's a growth bet. Which Approach Fits You

  • You want the most BlackSky in one fund: $NASA at 2.93%, plus the biggest asset base of the new space funds.
  • You want the lowest fee: $ORBX at 0.50%, with 1.81% BlackSky, but nearly a quarter of it is SpaceX.
  • You want SpaceX first and a little BlackSky on the side: $MARS. See my full list of ETFs that hold SpaceX.
  • You want the imaging theme without BlackSky: look at the funds holding its rival in my Planet Labs ETF list.
  • You want real BlackSky exposure on purpose: no ETF gives you much. That means buying the stock, and sizing it like a small speculative position.

My Take

I'm a dividend investor first. I reached financial freedom by building income, not by guessing which satellite company wins the next defense contract. But BlackSky is a great example of why I always look inside a fund before I buy it.

If you bought a space ETF because you liked the BlackSky earnings headline, you own about $160 to $290 of it per $10,000. You mostly own SpaceX. That's fine, just own it on purpose, and keep any single small-cap space stock to a size you can sit through a 60% drop with, because this one already did that this year.
