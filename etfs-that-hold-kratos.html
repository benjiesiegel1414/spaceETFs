Which ETFs Hold Kratos ($KTOS) Stock? The Full List for 2026

Kratos is in two ARK funds at about 5%, a big defense ETF at about 2% and a smaller slice of a fourth, but none of the pure space ETFs I track. Weights, fees, what the drone, hypersonics and satellite ground system company actually does, its latest numbers and the risks.

From Our Network
Quick answer: The ETFs that hold the most Kratos Defense & Security Solutions ($KTOS) are $ARKX (ARK Space & Defense Innovation ETF) at about 5.2% and $ARKQ (ARK Autonomous Technology & Robotics ETF) at about 4.9%, followed by $XAR (State Street SPDR S&P Aerospace & Defense ETF) at about 2.1% and $SHLD (Global X Defense Tech ETF) at about 0.9%. The pure space funds $UFO, $ORBX, $WARP, $NASA and $MARS don't show it in their top 25. Weights are as of November 19 to 20, 2026 and change daily.

My lists of ETFs that hold AST SpaceMobile, Rocket Lab and SpaceX keep pulling in readers, so I went looking for the next name people ask about. Kratos kept coming up. It's not a rocket company and it's not a satellite operator, but it sits right where space and defense overlap: it builds the ground software that commands and tracks satellites, plus jet drones, hypersonic test vehicles, rocket systems and the small jet engines that power missiles and drones.

It's also been a rough year for the stock. $KTOS closed at $26.89 on November 20, 2026, right near its 52-week low of $26.60 and far below its 52-week high of $134.00. It's down about 62% over the past 52 weeks, for a market value of about $5.06 billion. Here's every ETF that owns it, and how much you actually get.

The Four Funds, One by One

$ARKX ARK Space & Defense Innovation ETF

0.75% expense ~$588M AUM $KTOS 5.16%

The most Kratos of any ETF I found, 5.16%, and its seventh biggest holding. $ARKX launched March 30, 2021 and is actively managed. Its top holdings are SpaceX (11.89%), L3Harris (8.00%), Deere (6.23%), Rocket Lab (5.79%), Amazon (5.48%) and AeroVironment (5.20%), with Kratos right behind. If you want Kratos alongside a SpaceX and Rocket Lab position, this is the cleanest single-fund way to get it. I compared it with the other big space funds in $UFO vs $ARKX vs $ROKT.

$ARKQ ARK Autonomous Technology & Robotics ETF

0.75% expense ~$3.93B AUM $KTOS 4.85%

Kratos is 4.85% and the third biggest holding, behind Tesla (13.72%) and SpaceX (10.81%), and just ahead of Nvidia (4.67%). $ARKQ is the oldest fund here, launched September 30, 2014, and the biggest of the two ARK funds by far. It's a robotics and autonomy fund first, so think of Kratos as its drone and autonomous systems bet. If robotics is more your thing than space, my sister site RobotETFs.com covers that whole category.

$XAR State Street SPDR S&P Aerospace & Defense ETF

0.35% expense ~$4.75B AUM $KTOS 2.05%

Kratos is 2.05% as of November 19, 2026. $XAR spreads its money much more evenly than a typical cap-weighted fund: its biggest holding, Lockheed Martin, is only 3.53%, and the fund holds about 50 names. That makes it the cheapest way on this list to get some Kratos at 0.35% a year, but you're mostly buying the whole US aerospace and defense industry. Launched September 28, 2011.

$SHLD Global X Defense Tech ETF

0.50% expense ~$5.06B AUM $KTOS 0.92%

Kratos is 0.92%, number 21 in the fund. $SHLD is concentrated in the giants: Palantir (10.18%), RTX (9.89%), Lockheed Martin (9.72%), General Dynamics (9.16%) and Northrop Grumman (8.84%). Kratos is a small add-on here, not a reason to buy it. Launched September 11, 2023.

These funds don't show Kratos among their top 25 holdings in StockAnalysis.com data checked November 22, 2026 (holdings updated November 19, 2026 for most of them). A tiny slice could still sit further down, but it would be smaller than the 25th position.

  • The pure space funds, $UFO, $ORBX, $WARP, $NASA and $MARS: no Kratos in the top 25. These are built around SpaceX, Rocket Lab and satellite operators, not defense contractors.
  • $ROKT (SPDR S&P Kensho Final Frontiers ETF): not in the top 25 (cutoff about 2.28%). It leans on big aerospace names like Lockheed Martin, RTX and Northrop Grumman.

  • $PPA (Invesco Aerospace & Defense ETF): not in the top 25 (cutoff about 0.84%).

This is the part most people miss. If you bought a "space ETF" thinking you'd own the companies behind satellite ground systems and missile tech, you mostly don't. Kratos lives in the space and defense hybrids and the robotics funds. For the side by side on the new space funds, see my $NASA vs $MARS vs $ORBX vs $WARP breakdown.

What You're Actually Buying With Kratos

Kratos is based in San Diego, has about 4,300 employees and reports two segments. Here's what each brought in during the third quarter of 2026 (the quarter ended September 27, 2026):

  • Kratos Government Solutions, $397.2 million (up 52.5%, 35.8% organic): the big engine. This includes its space and satellite business (virtualized ground systems that command, track and control satellites), microwave electronics, turbine engines, defense rocket systems and hypersonics, plus training and cyber. The fastest organic growth was in Defense Rocket Systems at 149.0%. Space, Training and Cyber grew 11.6% organically.
  • Kratos Unmanned Systems, $74.6 million (down from $87.2 million): jet powered drones like the Valkyrie and target drones. Kratos says last year's number included a $16.1 million Valkyrie shipment to an international customer.

The space piece matters for a site like this one. Kratos says its Space and Satellite business has a significant new opportunity pipeline, and that its bid and proposal pipeline rose to $15.5 billion, with the most notable increases in space, training and hypersonics. On November 12, 2026 it also announced a portable terminal for space domain awareness under its Knownspace line.

Here's how I read it. The business numbers are strong: revenue up 35.7%, a record $2.65 billion backlog, more than twice as many new orders as revenue last quarter, and a 2027 forecast that calls for roughly 18% to 22% more growth. But the stock is down about 62% in a year anyway. That's the gap you have to be comfortable with. The company is still burning cash as it builds factories and inventory for drones, engines and hypersonics, it barely breaks even on a GAAP basis, and the share count is up about 13% in a year.

The Risks I'd Think About

  • Volatility: a 52-week range of $26.60 to $134.00. It fell 9.31% on November 20, 2026 alone.
  • Government funding: almost all of its revenue depends on US and allied defense budgets. Kratos itself says US defense funding is running under an extended Continuing Resolution, and it expects Q1 2027 to be its lightest quarter because of it.
  • Cash burn: free cash flow was negative $29.4 million in the quarter, and Kratos expects heavy spending on production capacity to keep weighing on cash flow.
  • Thin profits: a GAAP net loss of $0.2 million on $471.8 million of revenue. Adjusted numbers look better, but they leave out stock compensation and amortization.
  • Dilution: shares outstanding rose about 13.4% over the past year.
  • Fund concentration: even in $ARKX, Kratos is about 5%. If SpaceX, Rocket Lab or Tesla fall, that will move these funds a lot more than Kratos will.

Does Kratos Pay a Dividend?

No. Kratos doesn't pay a dividend as of November 2026, and none of the four ETFs above is built for income. That's the trade-off with the whole space and defense tech theme: you're betting on growth and accepting big swings. Which Approach Fits You

  • You want the most Kratos in one fund: $ARKX at 5.16%, with SpaceX and Rocket Lab as the anchors.
  • You want Kratos inside a robotics and autonomy fund: $ARKQ at 4.85%, where it's the third biggest holding.
  • You want the lowest fee and broad defense exposure: $XAR at 2.05%, with a 0.35% expense ratio.
  • You want the defense giants with a little Kratos on the side: $SHLD at 0.92%.
  • You want real Kratos exposure on purpose: buy $KTOS directly and size it like a single stock, not a fund. None of these ETFs gives you more than about 5%.

My Take

I'm a dividend investor first. I reached financial freedom by building income, not by guessing which defense contractor wins the next drone program. But Kratos is a good example of why I always look inside a fund before buying it. Most people would assume a "space ETF" owns the satellite ground system makers. For Kratos, the real exposure is in two ARK funds and a defense ETF.

The company's orders and backlog look like a business that's growing fast. The stock looks like one investors have lost patience with. Both can be true for a while. If you own $ARKX or $ARKQ, you already own about 5% Kratos, so I wouldn't stack a big direct position on top of that without knowing it. And keep any single stock like this to a size you can hold through another 50% drop, because it just did that.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which ETF has the most Kratos stock?

The ARK Space & Defense Innovation ETF ($ARKX) had the biggest Kratos weight at about 5.16% in ARK's November 20, 2026 holdings file, followed by the ARK Autonomous Technology & Robotics ETF ($ARKQ) at about 4.85%, the State Street SPDR S&P Aerospace & Defense ETF ($XAR) at about 2.05% as of November 19, 2026, and the Global X Defense Tech ETF ($SHLD) at about 0.92%.

Do space ETFs like UFO or ROKT hold Kratos?

Not in a meaningful way. The Procure Space ETF ($UFO), Global X Space Tech ETF ($ORBX), VanEck Space ETF ($WARP), Tema Space Innovators ETF ($NASA), Roundhill Space & Technology ETF ($MARS) and SPDR S&P Kensho Final Frontiers ETF ($ROKT) did not show Kratos among their top 25 holdings in StockAnalysis.com data checked November 22, 2026.

Does ITA hold Kratos?

The iShares U.S. Aerospace & Defense ETF ($ITA) did not show Kratos among its top 25 holdings in StockAnalysis.com data checked November 22, 2026, where the 25th position was about 0.46%. So if it holds Kratos at all, it's a very small slice. $XAR at about 2.05% is the bigger aerospace and defense option for Kratos.

What does Kratos do?

Kratos Defense & Security Solutions builds virtualized ground systems that command and track satellites, jet powered drones like the Valkyrie, hypersonic and rocket systems, small jet engines for missiles and drones, and microwave electronics. In the third quarter of 2026 it reported revenue of $471.8 million, up 35.7%, and a record backlog of $2.650 billion.

Does Kratos pay a dividend?

No. Kratos Defense & Security Solutions does not pay a dividend as of November 2026.
