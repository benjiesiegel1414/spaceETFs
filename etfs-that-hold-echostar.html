Which ETFs Hold EchoStar ($ECHO) Stock? The Full List for 2026

Two space funds hold real EchoStar positions, two more hold a few percent, and the big sector funds hold a sliver. Weights, fees, the $SATS to $ECHO ticker change, and why EchoStar doubles up your SpaceX bet.

Quick answer: The ETF with the biggest EchoStar ($ECHO) position is $WARP (VanEck Space ETF) at about 6.1%, its 3rd largest holding. Next is $UFO (Procure Space ETF) at about 5.4%. $NASA holds about 3.3% and the telecom fund $IYZ about 3.2%. The big communication services funds hold less: $XLC about 1.8%, $FCOM about 0.8% and $VOX about 0.7%. $MARS, $ORBX, $ROKT and the ARK funds don't show it in their top 25. Weights are as of early November 2026 and change daily.

My lists of ETFs that hold SpaceX, Viasat and AST SpaceMobile keep getting found on Google. And every time I pulled holdings for those, one name kept popping up near the top of the space funds: EchoStar.

If you're searching for "SATS ETF" and getting confused, you're not alone. EchoStar changed its Nasdaq ticker from $SATS to $ECHO on June 24, 2026. Same company, same CUSIP, new symbol. The old $SATS ticker now belongs to a completely different company's preferred stock, so make sure you're looking at $ECHO.

EchoStar is the parent of DISH TV, Sling TV, Boost Mobile and Hughes. It's also become one of the more interesting SpaceX side doors in the market, which I'll get to. $ECHO closed at $94.62 on November 6, 2026 and was trading near $100 on November 9. The 52-week range is $65.76 to $147.25, so this is not a sleepy telecom stock. Here's every way to own it in a fund.

The most EchoStar of any fund I found, 6.09%, and its 3rd biggest holding behind SpaceX (21.41%) and Rocket Lab (13.01%). After EchoStar come Globalstar (5.46%), MDA Space (5.22%) and Viasat (5.20%). $UFO Procure Space ETF

The oldest pure space fund, launched April 11, 2019, with about $416 million in assets and 67 holdings. EchoStar is its 4th largest position at 5.37%. This is the "real revenue" space fund: lots of companies already making money from satellites, not just launch hopes.

$NASA Tema Space Innovators ETF

The biggest of the new 2026 space funds at about $796 million and 39 holdings. EchoStar is 3.28%, its 11th largest. You get about half the EchoStar of $WARP and more SpaceX.

The Cheap Way: Telecom and Sector Funds

Here's where EchoStar is different from most names on my space lists. It's a big company now, roughly a $28 billion market cap, so it also shows up in plain vanilla sector funds.

$IYZ iShares U.S. Telecommunications ETF

0.37% expense ~$530M AUM $ECHO 3.24%

About the same EchoStar weight as $NASA (3.24%), at half the fee and with zero SpaceX. It's a concentrated telecom fund with just 26 holdings. If you want EchoStar as a wireless and connectivity story rather than a space story, this is the cleanest option on the list.

$XLC, $FCOM and $VOX

0.08% to 0.09% expense $ECHO 0.69% to 1.83%

$XLC holds about 1.83%, $FCOM about 0.82% and $VOX about 0.69%. These are huge, cheap funds ($XLC alone is about $21 billion), but they're dominated by Meta and Alphabet. On $10,000 in $XLC you own about $183 of EchoStar and about $2,078 of Meta. If you already own one of these, you already own a little EchoStar. Just don't buy one for that reason.

What You're Actually Buying With EchoStar

Before I buy a fund for one name, I want to know what that name actually is. Quick version:

  • The business: EchoStar is the parent of DISH TV, Sling TV, Boost Mobile and Hughes (HughesNet satellite internet). It merged back together with DISH Network in December 2023. Founder Charlie Ergen is CEO and Chairman.
  • The spectrum sales: in 2025 EchoStar agreed to sell big chunks of its wireless spectrum. About $23 billion to AT&T, and about $17 billion to SpaceX for its AWS-4 and H-block licenses, paid with up to $8.5 billion in cash and up to $8.5 billion in SpaceX stock. A second SpaceX deal in November 2025 sold unpaired AWS-3 licenses for about $2.6 billion in SpaceX stock. The FCC approved the SpaceX and AT&T sales in May 2026.
  • The size: 2025 revenue was about $15.0 billion, down about 5% from 2024, with about 12,100 employees. At the November 6 close the stock was worth roughly $27.5 billion.
  • The latest news: in early November 2026, reports said SpaceX was in talks to buy Boost Mobile or use its network. Nothing was announced as of this writing, so I treat that as a headline, not a thesis.
  • The basics: headquartered in Englewood, Colorado, listed on Nasdaq as $ECHO (formerly $SATS). No dividend.

So EchoStar has turned from a struggling pay TV and wireless company into something closer to a holding company sitting on cash, satellites, a shrinking TV business and a SpaceX stake. That's a much more complicated story than "satellite stock," and it's why the stock swung from about $66 to $147 in a year.

Which Approach Fits You

  • "I want the most EchoStar for the lowest fee." $WARP, 6.09% at 0.50%. Just know it's a $35 million fund launched in May 2026.
  • "I want a space fund with a track record." $UFO, 5.37%, launched in 2019 and full of satellite companies that already make money.
  • "I want EchoStar, not SpaceX." $IYZ, 3.24% at 0.37% with no SpaceX in the top 25.
  • "I just want cheap, broad exposure." $XLC at 0.08%. You'll own about 1.8% EchoStar, which is basically a rounding error next to Meta and Alphabet.
  • "I own $MARS, $ORBX, $ROKT or $ARKX." You don't have meaningful EchoStar exposure. Just know it.

My Take

I'm a dividend investor first. I reached financial freedom by building income, not by betting on spectrum deals and space IPOs. EchoStar doesn't pay a dividend, and the business is in the middle of a full reinvention, so I wouldn't expect one.

If I wanted exposure, I'd keep it small and get it through a basket. For the most EchoStar per dollar of fees, $WARP. For a space fund with five years of history, $UFO. And if what I really wanted was the connectivity story without stacking more SpaceX on top, I'd look at $IYZ.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which ETF has the most EchoStar stock?

The VanEck Space ETF ($WARP) had the biggest EchoStar weight at about 6.09% as of November 7, 2026, its 3rd largest holding. The Procure Space ETF ($UFO) was next at about 5.37%, followed by the Tema Space Innovators ETF ($NASA) at about 3.28% and the iShares U.S. Telecommunications ETF ($IYZ) at about 3.24%.

Did EchoStar change its ticker from $SATS to $ECHO?

Yes. EchoStar's common stock began trading on Nasdaq under the new ticker $ECHO on June 24, 2026. The company's legal name and CUSIP did not change, and shareholders did not need to do anything. The old $SATS ticker is now used by a different company's preferred stock.

Does EchoStar own SpaceX stock?

EchoStar's September 2025 agreement to sell its AWS-4 and H-block spectrum licenses to SpaceX was valued at about $17 billion, made up of up to $8.5 billion in cash and up to $8.5 billion in SpaceX stock. A second deal announced in November 2025 sold its unpaired AWS-3 licenses to SpaceX for about $2.6 billion in SpaceX stock. The FCC approved the SpaceX and AT&T spectrum sales in May 2026.

Is EchoStar in the S&P 500?

EchoStar shows up in the State Street Communication Services Select Sector SPDR ETF ($XLC) at about 1.83% as of November 6, 2026, and $XLC only holds S&P 500 communication services stocks. Broad communication services funds like $FCOM (about 0.82%) and $VOX (about 0.69%) also hold it.

Does EchoStar pay a dividend?

No. EchoStar ($ECHO) does not pay a dividend. It closed at $94.62 on November 6, 2026, a market value of about $27.5 billion at that price, and a 52-week range of $65.76 to $147.25.
